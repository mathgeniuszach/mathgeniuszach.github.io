# Will [AI](/response/tech/ai/what-is-ai/) Replace People? The answer to the question is sort of and no, but not in the way you think. If we take a brief look at the past, there are no doubt people who have asked similar questions about earlier technology. Consider the following tech and the elements of human-only capabilities that was automated by technology in the past: - **The Printing Press: Scribal Handwriting and Copying - **The Sewing Machine**: Sewing and Clothing Creation - **The Radio**: Long Range Messenging and Communication - **The Vehicle**: Long Range Hiking and Traveling - **The Computer**: Algorithmic Reasoning, Evaluation, and Manual Labor - **The Internet**: In Person Logistics and Media Distribution Consider for a second - has any of these technologies actually replaced what they've automated? I hope that you'd have to agree the answer is a clear no. If anything - these tools have actually *increased* the capacity of what people in these professions can do, not replaced them. People still write by things by hand, love caligraphy, type stories on a computer, travel long distances to talk in person, backpack by foot, do math for the love of it, labor manually. Consider now the next logical step - [Artificial Intelligence](/response/tech/ai/what-is-ai). Will it replace people? No, but it will make some skills and jobs obsolete, replacing them with new ones. New skills will be gained while old skills will be lost - and there will *ALWAYS* be a market to do it the old-fashioned way. If you're worried about art and music, don't be. Art's always been more than the media itself - it's a story. To give an example, in 2021, an artist named Jens Haaning was given two canvases and over 500 thousand Danish kroner (over 80000 USD) worth of bills that the museum expected to be used within the artwork. Haaning decided to return the blank canvases to the museum and pocket the money, titleing the paintings, ["Take the Money and Run"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Take_the_Money_and_Run_(artwork)). He was later sued as he had signed a written agreement to return the money. But, think about the whole scenario for a second, a random picture generated by an AI has less story than a blank canvas. Could an AI create such a story? Possibly - but within this exact context, culture, and while remaining fully authentic? By no means. Humanity will forever have an authoritative, positional, and identity-based place tech will never touch. No matter how much anyone tries to sub in and automate out the people component, it won't happen - they'll just move the goalpost as it always has moved before.
